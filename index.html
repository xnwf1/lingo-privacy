Lingo Privacy Policy

Effective date: 7 October 2026

Lingo ("the app") is an app for capturing and reviewing English words, expressions and example sentences. It is developed by Nawaf Alnuayri ("I", "me"). This policy explains:

  • what information the app handles on your device and in your iCloud;
  • what information I may receive through Apple's TestFlight service or when you email me;
  • what you can do about it.

The short version

  • Lingo has no accounts or sign-in, no advertising, no tracking and no in-app analytics SDK.
  • What you save in Lingo is stored on your device. If your library is set up to sync with iCloud, it is also stored in your own private iCloud database. Lingo itself does not send your saved content to me or to any server I operate.
  • If you test a beta version through TestFlight, Apple automatically shares crash logs and usage information with me. Any feedback you choose to send also reaches me.
  • I do not sell your information or use it for advertising.

Part 1: Information handled inside Lingo

What you create in the app

Lingo stores the content you create and your learning activity so the app can work:

  • Your library: the words, expressions and other items you save, including their meanings, examples, your personal examples, notes, source names, tags and collections. It also includes items you add from Lingo's built-in catalog.
  • Learning activity: your review and practice history (ratings, dates and next-review intervals), the days you studied (used for your streak), and which item was shown as your Word of the Day each day. Study days and Word of the Day entries record the date and your device's time zone.
  • Drafts and edit history, which Lingo keeps so that changes stay consistent across your devices.

Where it is stored

On your device. Your library and activity are stored on your device.

In iCloud, when your library is set up to sync. If your library is set up to sync and you are signed in to iCloud, Lingo also stores it in your private iCloud database using Apple's CloudKit service, so it can sync across devices signed in to the same Apple Account.

  • Apple stores and handles this data under Apple's own privacy policy.
  • Records in your private iCloud database are not visible to me in Apple's CloudKit developer tools.
  • A library created by an earlier version of Lingo, before iCloud sync was available, stays on your device only and is not uploaded. Lingo's Settings shows when sync is off for your library.
  • If you are not signed in to iCloud, your data stays on your device.

Lingo also receives silent background notifications from Apple's CloudKit service. These only tell the app that newer data is available in your iCloud, so it can update.

Settings stored on your device

Lingo stores a small number of preferences on your device, for example:

  • your interface language and appearance;
  • whether the daily reminder is on, and your pinned collections;
  • an unfinished review session, so you can resume it;
  • which starter suggestions you have dismissed;
  • the date of your last backup export, and whether you have finished the introduction.

These preferences stay on your device and are not sent to me.

Daily reminder

If you turn on the daily reminder, Lingo asks your permission to show notifications. The reminder is scheduled on your device. It contains only general text, such as "Time to review", not your saved content, and it is not sent through any server I operate. You can turn it off in Lingo or in your device's notification settings.

Backups you export

You can choose to export a backup of your library as a file and save it wherever you choose, for example in the Files app. You can also choose a backup file to import. Lingo does not upload these files anywhere; what happens to an exported file depends on where you save or share it.

Built-in catalog

Lingo includes a built-in catalog of learning content stored inside the app. Browsing and searching it happens on your device, and your searches are not sent anywhere.

What Lingo does not do

Lingo does not:

  • ask for your name, email address or phone number;
  • use your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone;
  • use advertising or advertising identifiers;
  • include third-party analytics, crash-reporting or tracking tools.

Lingo itself does not send usage analytics to me. The only usage information I receive is what Apple shares through TestFlight, described below.

Part 2: Information I may receive

TestFlight beta testing

If you test a beta version of Lingo through Apple's TestFlight, Apple's TestFlight & Privacy terms apply.

Shared automatically. Apple automatically collects crash logs and usage information and shares them with me; testers cannot opt out of this. This can include, for example:

  • device model, operating system version, language and time zone;
  • the number of sessions and crashes;
  • crash logs.

Shared only if you choose. If you send feedback, Apple shares it with me. It may include screenshots and comments, and anything you choose to include in it, such as text you saved in Lingo.

Public-link testers are anonymous to me. If you joined the beta only through a public TestFlight link, you appear in my tester list without your name or email address. That stays true unless you choose to include identifying details yourself, for example in feedback, a screenshot or an email. If you were invited by email, Apple's TestFlight terms describe when your name and email may be shared with me.

How I use it. I use TestFlight information only to find and fix problems and to improve Lingo.

No sharing. I do not share raw TestFlight data with third parties, including external AI tools.

How long it is kept.

  • Apple's records: Apple keeps its own TestFlight records under Apple's terms. Apple states that it keeps feedback for one year, and may keep crash log and usage data until bugs are resolved.
  • My copies: I keep any copies I make of TestFlight diagnostics and feedback for no longer than 12 months.

Email

If you email me, I receive your email address and anything you include in the message, such as screenshots or text from your library. I use it only to reply to you and to improve Lingo. I keep support emails for no longer than 12 months.

Children

Lingo is a general learning app. It has no accounts and does not ask anyone for their name, age or contact details. If you believe a child has sent me personal information by email or through TestFlight feedback, contact me. I will delete the copies under my control.

Deleting your data

Inside Lingo. Deleting an item removes it from your library. If your library syncs with iCloud, the deletion also reaches your private iCloud copy and your other devices. To make sure the deletion reaches all of them, Lingo may keep a small deletion marker: an identifier, not the item's text.

Uninstalling Lingo. Uninstalling removes the data stored on that device. It does not delete the copy in your private iCloud database. That copy stays in your iCloud until you delete it separately, and may be restored if you install Lingo again with the same Apple Account.

Deleting Lingo's iCloud data. You can manage the data stored in your iCloud from your device's iCloud storage settings. Apple explains this in Manage your iCloud storage on your Apple device. If you need help removing Lingo's iCloud data, contact me.

Information you sent me. I will honor requests to delete the copies of your emails, feedback or diagnostics that are under my control. Records that Apple keeps for TestFlight are held by Apple under Apple's terms, and I can only delete the copies I control.
